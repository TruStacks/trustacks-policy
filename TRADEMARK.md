# TruStacks Trademark Policy

The **code** in this repository is open-source under [Apache 2.0](./LICENSE). The **TruStacks name and brand** are not. This document explains what use of the TruStacks name, logo, and related marks is permitted.

This is an initial policy. We expect to revise it as the project grows.

---

## What's a trademark of TruStacks

The following are trademarks of TruStacks:

- The word marks **"TruStacks"** and **"TruStacks-Policy"**
- The TruStacks logomark (when published)
- The product names **"TruStacks Coordinator,"** **"TruStacks DevOps Engineer,"** **"TruStacks Code Reviewer,"** **"TruStacks Baseline Security,"** **"TruStacks SOC2 Specialist,"** and the names of any future TruStacks-published agents
- Domain names **trustacks.com** and **trustacks.app**

---

## Permitted use

You may use the TruStacks name in the following ways without asking permission:

- **Truthful descriptive use.** "I contributed a framework pack to TruStacks." "Our team uses TruStacks for software delivery." "This project integrates with TruStacks." Standard nominative fair use.
- **Linking to the project.** Hyperlinks to https://trustacks.com or to repositories under github.com/TruStacks.
- **Citing in academic, journalistic, or analyst writing.** Standard fair-use rules apply.
- **Internal references in your own documentation.** "We deploy with TruStacks" in your runbook is fine.

---

## Use that requires permission

The following uses of the TruStacks name require **written permission from TruStacks** before you proceed:

- **Naming a product, service, or company that includes "TruStacks" or a confusingly similar variant.** This includes domain names, app names, and corporate names.
- **Using the TruStacks logo or brand assets in marketing materials, product UIs, or public presentations.** Even non-commercial uses (talks, blog posts featuring the logo prominently) should ask first.
- **Distributing a fork of this repository under a name that suggests it is the official TruStacks distribution** (e.g., "TruStacks Plus," "Official TruStacks Distribution"). Forks should make clear they are independent of the TruStacks project.
- **Running a paid service or hosted product called "TruStacks"** (or with TruStacks in the name) — even if the underlying code is Apache 2.0 licensed.

For permission requests, email **trademark@trustacks.com**. We aim to respond within 10 business days.

---

## What we won't permit

- Use of the TruStacks name in a way that implies endorsement, partnership, or certification when none exists.
- Use of the TruStacks name in a domain name or product name that is likely to confuse end users.
- Use of the TruStacks logo as a generic decoration in materials unrelated to the TruStacks project.

---

## Forks

You're welcome to fork this repository under Apache 2.0. Forks should:

- Use a fork name that does not include "TruStacks" or a confusingly similar variant.
- Make clear in their README that they are an independent fork, not the official TruStacks project.
- Not use the TruStacks logomark.

The fork's *content* — rego rules, framework packs, documentation — remains Apache 2.0 and may be used under that license's terms. Only the *naming* and *branding* are restricted.

---

## Why this matters

We keep the TruStacks brand under our control because brand is the durable signal of trust for an open-core project: when a customer adopts something called "TruStacks" they need to know what they're getting and who stands behind it. The Apache 2.0 license on the code preserves the freedom to use, modify, and redistribute the work. The trademark policy preserves the integrity of the name.

This is the same model used by Kubernetes, Linux, Python, ArgoCD, sigstore, and most large open-source projects that have a trademark to protect.
